# Unreached — Editorial & Prayer Standard

**Phase:** U0  
**Status:** Baseline standard  
**Purpose:** govern original explanatory and prayer content so the site remains accurate, respectful, useful, and explicitly Christian.

---

## 1. Editorial objective

Unreached should help a reader answer five questions about a people group:

1. Who are they?
2. Where do they live?
3. What language(s) and religious traditions shape their context?
4. What helps explain their limited access to the gospel?
5. How can Christians pray for them intelligently?

The product should not attempt to summarize every aspect of a culture or country.

---

## 2. Required profile sections

A mature people-group profile should use the following order where sufficient evidence exists:

### Identity

- canonical display name
- aliases where useful
- country/countries
- region
- population estimate
- primary language
- primary religion
- source-defined mission status

### Who are they?

A concise original summary of identity, geography, cultural background, and relevant history.

### Language

- primary language
- language family where reliably sourced
- major related languages where useful
- script where relevant
- Scripture/resource indicators

### Religion and community context

Describe the dominant religious context factually. Avoid treating nominal affiliation as uniform individual belief.

### Gospel access

Explain available mission indicators with definitions and uncertainty.

### Why are they unreached?

Evidence-backed contextual synthesis. This is interpretation, not a raw dataset field.

### Why pray?

A short Christian bridge from understanding to intercession.

### What to pray for

Concrete, non-sensational prayer prompts grounded in available facts and biblical themes.

### Sources

Visible provenance for both imported data and material editorial claims.

---

## 3. Evidence levels

Every material editorial claim belongs to one of three levels.

### Level A — directly sourced fact

Examples:

- population estimate
- primary language
- legislation restricting religious activity
- documented displacement
- published Scripture status

Write directly but preserve uncertainty where the source does.

### Level B — multi-source synthesis

Examples:

- “Religious identity is closely connected to ethnic identity in many communities.”
- “Geographic dispersion and migration complicate sustained local-language ministry.”

Use only when supported by multiple credible facts/sources. Phrase cautiously.

### Level C — inference or hypothesis

Examples:

- attributing low Christian adherence to a single cultural trait;
- claiming a population is “resistant” because of a religion;
- predicting receptivity.

Level C claims should normally **not** be published. If a limited inference is genuinely useful, it must be explicitly framed as interpretation and backed by evidence.

---

## 4. Source expectations for editorial claims

### Stable cultural/history claims

Prefer:

1. academic publications
2. reputable encyclopedic/reference works
3. official cultural/institutional sources
4. recognized mission research organizations

### Current political/legal claims

Prefer:

1. primary law/government source when interpretable
2. recognized international organizations
3. reputable human-rights/religious-freedom reporting
4. multiple high-quality news sources

Current claims should include a date or temporal framing when circumstances can change.

### Mission-status claims

Use the designated mission-data provider and name the provider. Do not mix competing classifications invisibly.

---

## 5. Writing style

### Use

- concrete nouns
- short explanations
- cautious causal language
- source-aware wording
- present-day terminology used by the people themselves where available
- alternative names where necessary for discoverability

### Avoid

- “primitive”
- “backward”
- “exotic”
- “dark people/land” as cultural description
- “hostile religion”
- “resistant people” as an essentialized identity
- romanticized poverty
- treating non-Christians as statistical mission objects
- describing an entire people as though every member believes or behaves the same way

---

## 6. Naming people groups

Names may vary among datasets and communities.

Rules:

1. Preserve the source identifier separately from display name.
2. Prefer a widely recognized self-name/endonym where practical and unambiguous.
3. Store important aliases for search.
4. Do not silently merge groups solely because names look similar.
5. Do not silently split groups based on editorial assumptions.
6. For disputed or potentially offensive labels, document the naming decision.

---

## 7. Religion descriptions

Religion is essential context but must be handled carefully.

### Required distinction

Aggregate affiliation is not equivalent to personal conviction or practice.

### Allowed

- describe dominant traditions
- explain historical role
- describe documented social/legal dynamics
- note internal diversity when relevant
- describe Christian presence with the same caution

### Avoid

- caricatures of theology
- treating all adherents as identical
- implying that another religion makes a people less valuable
- describing conversion pressure without evidence
- presenting Christian mission categories as neutral demographic categories without explanation

---

## 8. “Why unreached?” template

This section should be assembled from relevant dimensions rather than forcing the same narrative onto every group.

Possible dimensions:

### Local church presence

What is known about indigenous Christian communities and their capacity?

### Language and media

Are Scripture and Christian resources available in languages people actually use and understand?

### Social identity

Are ethnicity, family, caste/community, or religious affiliation strongly interconnected in ways that create social costs for religious change?

### Geography

Are communities remote, dispersed, nomadic, transnational, or difficult to access?

### Legal/political context

Are there documented restrictions on proselytism, conversion, assembly, visas, or religious publishing?

### Conflict/displacement

Does instability disrupt ordinary community life and sustained ministry?

### Historical context

Have particular historical encounters shaped public perceptions of Christianity?

### Access gap

Is the key issue a lack of local witness, limited resources, language mismatch, or some other documented gap?

No profile needs every category.

---

## 9. “Why unreached?” prohibited shortcuts

Do not publish reasoning equivalent to:

- “They are Muslim, therefore unreached.”
- “Their culture rejects Christianity.”
- “They are spiritually resistant.”
- “Government X hates Christians” without precise evidence.
- “There are no believers” solely because a rounded field is zero.
- “No one has heard the gospel” unless a source can actually support that strong claim.

Prefer concrete wording such as:

- “The source estimates a very small evangelical presence.”
- “Public evangelistic activity is legally restricted.”
- “Most available Christian resources are not in the community’s primary language.”
- “Reliable information about indigenous church presence is limited.”

---

## 10. Prayer theology and tone

Unreached is explicitly Christian. Prayer prompts may therefore directly ask God for:

- the gospel to be heard and understood;
- people to come to faith in Jesus Christ;
- believers to mature in Scripture and holiness;
- churches to become healthy and locally rooted;
- faithful workers to be sent;
- Scripture to be translated and understood;
- justice, peace, protection, and wise government;
- reconciliation and human flourishing.

Prayer prompts should be biblically shaped but not imply that a particular contemporary political strategy or mission method has divine authority.

---

## 11. Prayer prompt construction

A normal people-group prayer set should include 4–7 prompts selected from these categories:

### Gospel

Pray for meaningful opportunities to hear and understand the gospel.

### Believers

Pray for faithfulness, courage, holiness, fellowship, and maturity among local Christians.

### Church

Pray for healthy, reproducing, indigenous churches grounded in Scripture.

### Scripture and resources

Pray for accurate translation, distribution, literacy/orality-appropriate resources, and comprehension.

### Workers

Pray for wise, humble, culturally attentive, persevering Christian workers.

### Community wellbeing

Pray for peace, justice, protection of the vulnerable, family wellbeing, and freedom from violence or exploitation.

### Authorities

Pray for leaders and governments to act justly and permit peaceful religious life.

### Specific documented needs

Where a group faces displacement, conflict, disaster, repression, or other major current conditions, prayer may address them with current sourcing.

---

## 12. Scripture references in prayer mode

Scripture references may support prayer prompts, but should not become random decorative verses.

Good uses:

- Matthew 9:37–38 — workers
- Romans 10:13–17 — hearing and proclamation
- Colossians 4:2–4 — open doors and clarity
- 2 Thessalonians 3:1–2 — spread of the word and protection
- Ephesians 3:14–21 — maturity and spiritual strength
- 1 Timothy 2:1–4 — authorities and peace
- Revelation 7:9–10 — worship from every people and language

Do not use verses as proof texts for unsupported factual claims about a people.

---

## 13. Sensitive prayer content

Never publish a prompt that identifies:

- a covert believer by name;
- an underground congregation’s precise location;
- a missionary’s hidden identity;
- a private legal case not already responsibly public;
- a conversion story whose publication could endanger the person.

Public prayer can remain specific in need without being specific in identity.

---

## 14. Images of people

Photographs of people carry special ethical weight even when copyright permits reuse.

Rules:

1. Prefer images genuinely connected to the people/context.
2. Do not use generic “poverty” imagery to manufacture urgency.
3. Avoid humiliating or voyeuristic images.
4. Check license and personality/privacy concerns.
5. Never imply that a pictured individual personally holds a religion, belief, or mission status unless that is actually known and appropriate to state.
6. Captions should describe the image, not assign hidden beliefs to subjects.

---

## 15. Maps

Maps should communicate approximate geographic context, not false precision.

For people-group locations:

- use generalized areas/points when that is what the source provides;
- explain that a point can represent an approximate center of a wider population;
- avoid precision that could expose vulnerable communities;
- avoid implying ethnographic homogeneity inside administrative boundaries.

---

## 16. Review checklist for each editorial profile

Before publication:

- [ ] Identity and naming checked.
- [ ] Imported statistics have provenance.
- [ ] Unknowns are not guessed.
- [ ] Current claims are current enough for publication.
- [ ] “Why unreached?” uses evidence rather than stereotypes.
- [ ] No monocausal explanation is presented as fact.
- [ ] Religion is described accurately and respectfully.
- [ ] Christian-adherent/Evangelical metrics are explained where shown.
- [ ] Prayer prompts are concrete and biblically appropriate.
- [ ] No sensitive identities/locations are exposed.
- [ ] Images have license metadata and are contextually appropriate.
- [ ] Material claims have citations.
- [ ] Source attribution requirements are satisfied.

---

## 17. Quality tiers

Not every imported group needs a full editorial profile immediately.

### Tier 0 — Data record

Verified imported facts only.

### Tier 1 — Prayer-ready

Data record + verified prayer prompts + short context.

### Tier 2 — Editorial profile

Full original contextual profile with citations and “Why unreached?” synthesis.

### Tier 3 — Reviewed feature profile

Editorial profile with additional human-style review for nuance, current context, media, and source quality.

The UI should not pretend Tier 0 data has the same explanatory depth as a reviewed Tier 3 profile.

---

## 18. AI-assisted editorial work

AI may assist the maintainer during content production, but generated output is never itself a source.

Rules:

- factual AI output must be verified against sources;
- citations must point to actual source material, not the AI;
- AI must not fabricate “Why unreached?” causes to fill missing fields;
- AI-generated prayer prompts must be reviewed for factual assumptions and tone;
- sensitive-context content requires extra scrutiny;
- automatic bulk publication of unreviewed generated narratives is prohibited.

This does not make an AI chatbot part of V1; it only governs behind-the-scenes authoring assistance.

---

## 19. Editorial success criterion

A good Unreached profile should leave the reader with more **specific understanding**, more **epistemic humility**, and more **concrete subjects for prayer** than a raw statistics page would provide.

If a section adds drama but not understanding, remove it.
